Build · Buy · Staff

The agent fleet — and why it still needs a human

2026 AI tooling can take over 60–80% of the minutes a back-office assistant spends, but under half the value. It bottoms out at a thin human layer that doesn't go to zero. Below is where things stand today, task by task.

🟢 Works now — off your plate

  • Appointment reminders & recall texts/calls Weave
  • Insurance eligibility / benefit calls — holds through the IVR mazes Infinitus
  • Inbound front-desk calls, reschedules, new-patient intake Assort / Weave
  • EOB / insurance-card / intake document reading Reducto / Extend
  • Non-PHI outbound (vendor, tenant, referral) Retell / Synthflow

🟡 Works supervised — agent does it, I own the misses

  • Claim status & billing follow-up — agent gathers, I decide & push the biller
  • Eligibility exceptions — the 1–2% of calls that come back wrong
  • Inbound calls that go off-script — warm-transferred to a human
  • Autonomous email send — drafted 100%, one-click human approve

⚫ Stays human — what you're actually paying a person for

  • Accountability. An agent can't own a mistake. Someone has to take responsibility and push the biller when something's wrong. That's human.
  • The silent-failure tail. Tools run at 95–99%, not 99.9%. In billing, the 5% shows up weeks later as denials. Someone has to own that queue.
  • The Square problem. The strong vertical agents assume a real EHR and don't touch Square, so connecting systems takes custom work. Worth deciding: stay on Square, or move to a PT-native EMR that allows more automation.
  • Running the fleet. 6–8 narrow vendors create a new supervision job. That job is the role.

So instead of hiring an assistant for the messy work and still managing them, you get one person who runs the fleet and owns the thin, high-skill remainder. Fewer people to manage, and it's clear who's accountable.

How we paper it

Two ways to structure the relationship

Pay and budget stay separate in both. What changes is how I'm engaged and who owns the software. The trade-offs:

My recommendation

Managed-service partner

I invoice monthly through my own company; I retain the software IP and operate it for you.

✓Cleanest budget-vs-pay split — one labor invoice, one at-cost stack line
✓Fastest to start — no payroll, benefits, or hiring process
✓You get the software as your edge without owning its maintenance
–You're buying an outcome + a seat, not an employee on your org chart

Full-time employee (COO)

Salary + benefits, on staff as Recalibrate's operations lead.

✓Simplest mental model — I'm "your COO," fully inside the business
–Software I build risks becoming your work-for-hire IP — kills reusing it elsewhere
–Payroll, benefits, and the budget has to live on the company's books separately

What I need from you to firm up the numbers

  • What system holds your clinical notes — so I know if we can connect it or need to move you off Square/Docs first.
  • Billing data access — can your biller hand over raw 835/ERA files or a clearinghouse login?
  • Square vs. PT-native EMR — staying on Square keeps a higher manual floor; worth deciding.
  • Which structure above feels right to you — that sets the paperwork.
